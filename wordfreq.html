Frequently asked questions

What counts as a word here?

A word is a run of letters and digits, optionally with an apostrophe inside it, so “don’t” counts as one word and “well-known” counts as two (well, known). Numbers count as words too. Punctuation and symbols only separate words.

What are stop words and why ignore them?

Stop words are the very common words — the, of, and, a, in, to — that usually dominate any text’s frequency list without saying much about its topic. Turning on “ignore stop words” hides a built-in list of the most common ones so the meaningful words stand out. It is most useful together with case-insensitive counting.

How are the results sorted and the percentage calculated?

Words are sorted by count, most frequent first; words with the same count are listed alphabetically. The percentage is the word’s count divided by the total number of words counted (after any stop words are removed), shown to one decimal place.

Is my text uploaded anywhere?

No. The counting runs entirely in your browser with JavaScript. Your text never leaves your device.
