How to Get Ghibli Museum Tickets From Overseas

The sale happens once a month, at one exact moment in Japan time — and nothing is sold at the door. Here is how the system works, and what to do if your date is already gone.

You planned the Tokyo trip around it. You arrive at a small museum in a leafy park in Mitaka, walk up to the entrance — and learn the one fact that breaks more itineraries than any train strike: the Ghibli Museum sells no tickets at the door. None. Every visit is a dated, timed reservation bought in advance, and the whole of next month goes on sale on a single morning. This guide gives you the exact sale moment, how buying from overseas works, how the Museum differs from Ghibli Park, and the official Plan B routes.

The short answer

  • Ghibli Museum (Mitaka, Tokyo): tickets for the whole of next month go on sale at 10:00 JST on the 10th of this month, through Lawson Ticket. From overseas, use the English Lawson Ticket site linked from the museum's official website. Visiting in February? You buy on January 10.
  • Ghibli Park (Nagakute, Aichi) is a different place with a different clock: its tickets go on sale at 14:00 JST on the 10th, two months ahead. A Museum ticket is not valid at the Park, and the Park is roughly 300 km from Tokyo.
  • Tickets are personal. The booking is tied to the representative's name, ID (a passport for overseas visitors) is checked at entry, and resold or transferred tickets are refused. Never buy from resale platforms.
  • Since November 2026, Museum tickets are QR e-tickets shown on your smartphone, with a ¥110 system fee per ticket on top of admission. Screenshots do not work — you display the live QR code.

Museum or Park? Two venues, two systems

Confusing the two venues is the most expensive mistake in this whole process. They share a name and a world, but not a city, a sale day, or a ticket.

Step 1: Work backwards from your visit month

Tickets for an entire month are released at once: 10:00 JST on the 10th of the previous month. A visit any time in April means buying on March 10. Check the museum's official calendar first — the museum closes on Tuesdays and for maintenance and holiday periods, and those closed days simply have no tickets to buy.

Step 2: Convert 10:00 JST into your time — carefully

Japan does not observe daylight saving time; your city probably does. The same sale falls at a different local time in winter and summer:

Set two alarms — one the day before, one 30 minutes before the sale — and note the date, not just the hour: in the Americas, the sale is on the evening of the 9th.

Step 3: Prepare before the 10th

Start from the ticket page of the museum's official website (ghibli-museum.jp) and follow its link to the English Lawson Ticket site — fake look-alike sites exist, so never arrive via a random search ad. Have ready: the representative's name exactly as in their passport, a passport for entry day, a payment card, and your ranked dates (Step 4). Entry is in hourly slots from 10:00 to 16:00, and you must arrive within your slot's hour.

Admission is ¥1,000 for ages 19 and over, ¥700 for ages 13–18, ¥400 for ages 7–12, and ¥100 for ages 4–6; children aged 3 and under are free. Add the ¥110-per-ticket system fee at checkout.

Step 4: Rank your dates before the sale opens

Weekend and holiday slots go first. Before 10:00, write down three choices:

  1. First choice: your ideal date and earliest acceptable entry time.
  2. Second choice: the nearest weekday — Tuesdays excluded, the museum is closed — or a later entry time on the same day.
  3. Third choice: any remaining date in your trip, even if it reshuffles another plan.

During the sale, work down the list without stopping to deliberate. A confirmed second choice beats a perfect date that sold out while you were thinking.

Step 5: On the day — buy, then protect the QR code

For visits from November 2026, the ticket is a QR e-ticket: after booking, you display the QR code on your smartphone at the entrance. A screenshot or printout will not get you in; you need an internet connection and the booking representative with their passport at the gate. Save the booking emails and charge your phone.

Step 6: If the Museum sells out — the official Plan B routes

  • Sunrise Tours (JTB): the museum's official site lists this JTB bus tour as the other overseas route. It bundles Museum entry with a guided day — a different price and experience, but a genuine official allocation when Lawson is sold out.
  • Switch the Ghibli day to Ghibli Park: if your trip passes Nagoya or you can spare a day, the Park's sale is calmer — but remember its clock is different and it is a separate trip, not a Tokyo substitute. Park tickets for a whole month go on sale at 14:00 JST on the 10th, two months before that month — February entry sells on December 10.
  • What does not work: resale sites, auction listings, and "guaranteed ticket" agents. Resold tickets are invalid and refused at entry, however official the listing looks.

FAQ

Can I buy a Ghibli Museum ticket at the entrance on the day?

No. Nothing is sold at the door, ever. Every visit is a dated, timed reservation bought in advance through the monthly sale.

What if I set my alarm for 10:00 on the 10th in my own time zone?

You will likely miss the sale. It is 10:00 Japan time — in New York that is the evening of the 9th. Convert the time for your sale month, allowing for daylight saving, and note the local date.

Can I use a screenshot of my QR ticket?

No. Since November 2026 the Museum ticket is a live QR display on your smartphone. Arrive with the actual booking accessible on your phone, an internet connection, and the representative's passport.

Is a second-hand ticket a safe backup?

No. Tickets are name-bound and resold tickets are refused at the entrance. There is no legitimate resale market.
