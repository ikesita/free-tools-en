The 160 cm Rule: Shinkansen Oversized Baggage Without the ¥1,000 Mistake

One measurement — height + width + depth, wheels and handle included — decides whether your suitcase needs a special seat. Here is how to measure it, which lines require the free reservation, and which lines have no such rule at all.

You bought the big suitcase for this trip. It swallowed two weeks of clothes, the souvenirs are already multiplying, and tomorrow you ride the Shinkansen from Tokyo to Kyoto. Then, the night before, you see a forum post: “Wait — do I need a special seat for a suitcase this size?” You do not have a tape measure. The hotel front desk is closed. And the answer turns out to depend on three numbers you have never added together.

This is the most repeated luggage question in Japan travel forums, because the rule is simple but invisible: nothing at the station warns you until you are already on board. This guide gives you the measurement, the lines it applies to, and the booking step that takes two minutes — so you never pay the ¥1,000 on-board fee for a reservation that was free.

The short answer

Add your suitcase’s three outside dimensions together: height + width + depth, in centimeters. That single total decides everything.

Total of 3 sidesWhat it means
160 cm or lessNormal baggage. No reservation, no fee. Overhead rack, at your feet, or in a deck storage area.
Over 160 cm, up to 250 cm“Oversized baggage.” On the Tokaido, Sanyo, Kyushu and Nishi-Kyushu Shinkansen you must book a seat with an oversized baggage area in advance. The seat costs the same as any reserved seat — the baggage space itself is free.
Over 250 cmCannot be brought on board at all, on any Shinkansen. Ship it by luggage delivery instead.

Board an oversized bag on those four lines without the reservation and the conductor charges a ¥1,000 carry-on fee (tax included) and moves your bag to a spot the crew designates. You are not fined for the size of the bag — you are charged for skipping a free booking.

Step 1: Measure the outside of the case — wheels and handles included

Measure the case at its outermost points: from the floor to the top of the (collapsed) handle, across the widest part of the body, and from front to back including the wheels and any protruding handle or pocket. Then add the three numbers.

Why the outside? The rule is about the physical space the bag occupies behind the seat, so what counts is the space your case actually takes up, not the manufacturer’s “capacity” figure or the height of the shell alone. Manufacturer size charts often list the body without wheels — a case sold as just under the line on paper can measure over it in real life. If you have no tape measure, hotel front desks will usually lend you one, and many large stations have size-check displays near the Shinkansen gates.

The 28-inch trap. The big checked suitcases sold as “28-inch” or “L-size” sit right on this border. Depending on the model, the same nominal size can total under 160 cm or several centimeters over it — shell shape, wheel size and handle design all shift the total. Never assume from the inch label. Measure your case, with your wheels and handle, and trust that number.

Step 2: Check which Shinkansen you are riding

  • Reservation required: Tokaido (Tokyo–Shin-Osaka), Sanyo (Shin-Osaka–Hakata), Kyushu (Hakata–Kagoshima-Chuo), Nishi-Kyushu (Takeo-Onsen–Nagasaki). One through trip can cross all of them — the same rule follows you.
  • No reservation system: Tohoku, Hokkaido, Joetsu, Hokuriku, Yamagata, Akita (JR East and JR Hokkaido).
  • Ordinary (non-Shinkansen) trains: no oversized-baggage reservation exists. The general carry-on limits below still apply.

This is where travelers most often go wrong in the other direction: Tokyo–Kyoto needs the booking; Tokyo–Sendai or Tokyo–Kanazawa does not. A rule learned on one route does not transfer to the other.

Step 3: Book the seat with an oversized baggage area

Book it when you buy the ticket — it cannot be added as an afterthought at the platform. You can book online via Smart-EX / the Tokaido–Sanyo–Kyushu online reservation services, JR-WEST ONLINE TRAIN RESERVATION, or JR-KYUSHU Train Reservation — choose the seat type marked “seat with an oversized baggage area.” At a station, use a reserved-seat ticket machine (select the same seat type) or a ticket office and tell the staff you are traveling with oversized baggage. With a JAPAN RAIL PASS, reserve the same seat type at a ticket office or via the pass reservation site; there is no extra charge.

The seat itself is the last row of the car, and the space directly behind it is yours for the bag. These seats are limited — a small share of each train — so on busy routes and holidays, book early. Two consequences follow: oversized baggage cannot be taken into non-reserved cars, and it cannot go in an ordinary reserved car that has no oversized-baggage seats. If those seats are sold out on your train, the official options are a different train, or shipping the bag ahead.

At a ticket office, you can show this: 特大荷物スペースつき座席を予約したいです — “I’d like to reserve a seat with an oversized baggage area.”

Good to know: strollers, musical instruments, sports equipment and wheelchairs are exempt from this reservation rule even when they total over 160 cm (keep them in their case or bag where applicable). You only need the special seat if you want to use the space behind the last row for them.

Step 4: On board — and if you got it wrong

With the reservation, slide the case into the space behind your seat and you are done; the space is shared among the passengers who booked that row, so pack it considerately. Without the reservation, expect the crew to charge ¥1,000 and direct the bag to a designated place — and on a full train, that may mean a worse outcome than the fee: there may simply be no good place for it. The fee is avoidable in full, every time, by booking ahead.

Step 5: The JR East difference, and the hard limits

On the Tohoku, Joetsu, Hokuriku and other JR East Shinkansen, there is no 160 cm reservation threshold. Any suitcase within the general carry-on limits rides free with no booking: use the overhead racks, the space at your feet, or the designated luggage storage areas fitted on many trains. The practical risks are different ones: storage areas are first-come, first-served, they fill up on peak trains, and a very large case may only fit in the designated areas — so board early and head straight for the storage space.

General carry-on limits (all Shinkansen): up to 2 pieces per person (handbags, umbrellas and canes don’t count), each piece 250 cm or less in total dimensions, longest side 2 m or less, and 30 kg or less per piece. Anything over these limits cannot be brought on board — use a luggage delivery service (takkyubin) for those.

FAQ

My suitcase is sold as “28-inch.” Is it over 160 cm?

Nobody can tell you from the label — models with the same nominal size land on both sides of the line. Measure the outside of your own case, wheels and handle included, and add the three sides.

Does a regular reserved seat count?

No. Only the specific seat type with an oversized baggage area counts. A normal reserved seat in a car without that space leaves you liable for the ¥1,000 fee.

Can I use a non-reserved car for one stop?

No. Oversized baggage is not permitted in non-reserved cars at all. With a bag over 160 cm on the four reservation lines, unreserved seating is off the table.

What if the oversized-baggage seats are sold out?

Take a different train, or ship the bag ahead by luggage delivery. There is no official fallback of using the space behind someone else’s seat.
