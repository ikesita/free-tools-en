Japan Tax-Free Shopping Changed on November 1, 2026: How the Airport Refund Works Now

Instant tax-free checkout is gone. If you shop in Japan now, you pay full price in the store — and the refund depends on one step at the airport, before you check your bags.

You are at the airport, bags already checked, boarding in an hour — and you remember the tax-free shopping. Under the rules most travel blogs still describe, that would not matter much: the tax came off at the register when you bought the goods. But for purchases made on or after November 1, 2026, the tax never came off. You paid the full tax-inclusive price in the store, and the only way to get the tax back is a customs confirmation at the airport — before you check your bags. Once your suitcase is on the belt, the goods inside it can no longer be confirmed, and the refund for that purchase is gone.

Japan replaced instant tax-free checkout with a refund system (the “refund method”) for all purchases made from November 1, 2026. Purchases made up to October 31, 2026 stay under the old system, even if you fly home later. This guide explains the new system: what changed, what did not, and the exact order of steps that protects your refund.

The short answer

  • In the store: show your passport, pay the full price including tax, and register how you want the refund paid. The method depends on the store or its refund operator — commonly a credit card or bank transfer.
  • At the airport: go to the tax-free procedure terminal (kiosk) in the international departure lobby, or use the Visit Japan Web flow where available, and scan your passport — before baggage check-in and before security.
  • The deadline: customs must confirm your goods within 90 days of each purchase date.
  • The minimum: still ¥5,000 before tax, at one store, on one day — unchanged. What changed is that all categories now count toward that one total.
  • What you get back: the consumption tax amount. At the 10% rate, that is 1/11 of the price you paid — not 10% of it — possibly minus a handling fee charged by the store or refund operator.

Two quieter changes matter at the airport. First, you may only buy quantities you can personally carry out of Japan — the goods must travel with you. Second, for a single item priced at ¥1,000,000 or more before tax, the store records detailed product information such as the brand, model and serial number, and customs may want to visually inspect that item. Pack expensive purchases where you can reach them.

Step 1: In the store — passport out, full price paid

Shop at a store displaying the tax-free sign, spend at least ¥5,000 before tax in that store on that day, and show your passport. You pay the tax-inclusive price like any local customer. The store sends your purchase record to the tax authorities electronically, and you register your refund method. Keep the receipt and any refund paperwork with your passport, not in the shopping bag.

Because categories are now pooled, a ¥3,000 jacket and ¥2,500 of cosmetics at the same store on the same day reach the minimum together — something the old rules did not allow. Useful phrases: Menzei de onegai shimasu (“Tax-free, please”) and Henkin hōhō o tōroku shitai desu (“I’d like to register my refund method”).

Step 2: Pack for the confirmation, not just for the flight

Customs confirmation is done per purchase record — effectively per receipt. If even one item from a receipt cannot be shown, none of the items on that receipt can be confirmed, and that whole purchase loses its refund. So keep everything you bought tax-free with you until after the confirmation — in your carry-on, or in the suitcase you have not checked yet. Do not eat, drink, or use consumables you bought tax-free before you leave; they must still be unused when you depart. Keep high-value items (¥1,000,000 and over) easy to reach, in case officers ask to see them.

Step 3: At the airport — kiosk first, check-in second

In the international departure lobby, find the tax-free procedure terminal and scan your passport. Do this before you drop your bags: after check-in, customs confirmation is no longer possible, and bags cannot be pulled back for the sake of the procedure. At seven major airports — Narita, Haneda, Kansai, Chubu, New Chitose, Fukuoka and Naha — the confirmation can also be completed through Visit Japan Web in designated areas of the departure lobby.

A green result means the confirmation is complete. A red result sends you to a customs inspection point, where officers check the goods against your purchase records. Either way, allow extra time: if you abandon the confirmation because your flight is boarding, it counts as never having been confirmed. Arriving 30–45 minutes earlier than usual on a shopping-heavy trip is a reasonable buffer. At the airport you can ask: Zeikan kakunin wa doko desu ka? (“Where is the customs confirmation for tax-free goods?”)

Step 4: The refund arrives afterwards — and the math

Once customs confirmation is recorded, the store — or the refund operator it uses — pays the tax amount back by the method you registered. Timing and method vary by operator. Many operators deduct a handling fee; published examples from major operators and department stores have ranged roughly from 1.55% to 2.2% of the purchase amount, but the fee is set by each business, so check the rate where you shop rather than assuming one.

Remember the math: on a ¥100,000 purchase at the 10% rate, the tax portion is ¥9,090 (1/11 of the price), and any fee comes out of that. Expecting “10% back” overstates the refund before fees are even considered.

Common mistakes

  • Checking your bags first. The single most expensive mistake under the new system. Confirmation happens before check-in, with the goods physically with you.
  • Splitting a receipt’s items across bags. Confirmation is per purchase record: one missing item voids the whole receipt’s refund.
  • Trusting pre-November 2026 blog posts. Instant tax-free checkout, category rules, the ¥500,000 consumables cap and sealed bags are all old-system details.
  • Using up the cosmetics on the trip. Consumables bought tax-free must leave Japan unused.

FAQ

Does the ¥5,000 minimum still apply?

Yes. It is still ¥5,000 before tax, at one store, on one day. The change is that general goods and consumables now count toward that total together, instead of separately.

How much do I actually get back?

The tax portion of what you paid: at the 10% rate, 1/11 of the tax-inclusive price (8/108 at the 8% food rate), minus any handling fee charged by the store or refund operator.

What if I bought things before November 1, 2026?

Purchases made up to October 31, 2026 stay under the old instant system, even if you fly home later. The refund system applies to purchases from November 1, 2026.

What happens if I skip the airport confirmation?

Without customs confirmation within 90 days of the purchase date, there is no refund for that purchase. Abandoning the procedure because your flight is boarding counts as never having been confirmed.
