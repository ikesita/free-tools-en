Send Your Suitcase Ahead in Japan: Takkyubin Luggage Delivery

Your suitcase doesn't have to travel with you. Japan's door-to-door delivery service forwards your luggage ahead of you, usually overnight — here is how it works, what it costs, and when to send it.

Every visitor to Japan has the same worst day: the day you change cities. A big suitcase on a crowded train, stairs in every station, coin lockers all full, and you arrive at the new hotel already exhausted. Japanese travelers solved this decades ago with takkyubin (宅急便) — a door-to-door delivery service that forwards your luggage ahead of you, usually overnight, for roughly the price of a station lunch set. Once you know it exists, moving day becomes the easiest day of the trip.

The short answer

Send your big suitcase from your hotel front desk the day before you move. It arrives at your next hotel the next day. Budget roughly ¥2,000–3,500 per large suitcase between major cities. Keep only tonight's essentials in a small day bag. Sending a bag to the airport for your flight home works too — but that one must go out at least 2 days before departure.

Step 1: Ask the front desk — the day before

Go to your hotel's front desk the day before checkout (morning or early afternoon is safest) and say: "Takkyubin de tsugi no hoteru ni nimotsu o okuritai desu" (宅急便で次のホテルに荷物を送りたいです — "I'd like to send my luggage to my next hotel by takkyubin"). Most hotels handle Yamato Transport (the black-cat company, クロネコヤマト) shipments at the desk. Two questions to settle on the spot: their drop-off cutoff time, and whether your next hotel accepts luggage before check-in. Big hotels almost always do; unstaffed rentals and many Airbnbs do not.

Sending on checkout morning is the classic mistake: miss the pickup cutoff and your bag travels a day behind you. The day before is the rule.

Step 2: Have the destination details ready

You will write these on the form, so pull up your booking confirmation first:

  • The next hotel's full official name
  • Its address and phone number
  • Your check-in date and the name on the reservation

Writing the hotel's name the way it appears on its Japanese website is even better — hotel names repeat a lot in Japan, and the address and phone number are what keep your bag out of trouble. Writing only a short English name is how bags get misdelivered.

Step 3: Fill in the waybill (English is fine)

The delivery slip — denpyō (伝票) — has three blocks:

BlockWhat to writeExample
お届け先 (Receiver)Next hotel's postal code, address, phone; hotel name + "c/o [your name], check-in [date]"〒604-8152 Kyoto… / Sakura Hotel Kyoto, c/o J. Smith, Check-in: Nov 25
ご依頼主 (Sender)Your current hotel's address and phone, and your nameHotel Sun Tokyo… / J. Smith
品名 (Contents)Be specific"Suitcase, clothes, shoes"

Use the sender-pays slip (the pink-toned one). You may write everything in English letters — that is normal. And don't be shy: front-desk staff fill these in with guests every day and will happily help you with yours.

No front desk? Takkyubin can also be sent from convenience stores and delivery company offices, where you fill in the same waybill yourself and pay at the counter. The trade-off is help: a hotel desk will check your next hotel's details, confirm the cutoff, and hand you the right slip, while at a convenience store you are on your own with the form. If it is your first time, the front desk is the easier route; if your accommodation has no desk, a convenience store drop-off with the destination's full name, address, and phone number written carefully works the same way once the slip is accepted.

Step 4: Pay, and photograph the receipt

Price is set by size — length + width + height of the bag in centimeters — and distance. A standard large suitcase is "size 160" (up to 160 cm total, 25 kg); Tokyo → Kyoto or Osaka runs about ¥2,600 and up at current rates (fares were revised in October 2025). Hotels often take cash only for this, even when the room went on a card. Your copy of the slip carries the tracking number — take a photo of it. That's your proof and your tracking in one.

Step 5: Sending a bag to the airport — the 2-day rule

For the trip home, Airport TA-Q-BIN delivers your suitcase to a counter at your departure airport. The rules that matter: ship it at least 2 days before your flight (3 days from some regions), there's a ¥660 airport fee per bag, and you collect it at the counter showing your passport and flight details. The reverse — airport counter to hotel when you arrive — also exists, but standard delivery is next-day (a few airports offer same-day to central Tokyo). Either way, pack one night's essentials in your carry-on. That small overnight bag is the whole secret to this system.

What you must never put in the case

  • Cash, passport, cards, valuables. Compensation is capped at ¥300,000 per parcel, and you can't open the bag in transit. Passports stay on your person, always — the safest place in the case is the worst place for a passport.
  • Dangerous goods: spray cans, lighter fuel, loose lithium batteries.
  • Perishables and fragile items — and anything you'll need tomorrow morning. That's what the day bag is for.

FAQ

How much does it cost to send a large suitcase?

Budget roughly ¥2,000–3,500 per large suitcase between major cities. Price is set by size (length + width + height in centimetres) and distance: a standard large suitcase is "size 160," and Tokyo → Kyoto or Osaka runs about ¥2,600 and up at current rates. The front desk will quote the precise figure before you pay.

Can I send my suitcase on the morning I check out?

You can try, but it is the most common mistake. Miss the pickup cutoff and your bag travels a day behind you. Ask the front desk the day before moving — not on checkout morning.

Can I ship a bag to my Airbnb?

Only if you confirm first. Unstaffed rentals often have no one to receive the bag, and it comes back. Confirm with your host first, or send it to a nearby hotel or delivery office instead.

How early must an airport delivery go out?

At least 2 days before your flight (3 days from some regions), with a ¥660 airport fee per bag. Collect it at the airport counter with your passport and flight details.
