Behind on Pension or NHI Payments in Japan? What to Fix Before Your Visa Renewal

Unpaid pension and National Health Insurance premiums have long been a debt problem. From June 2027 they are expected to matter at immigration too — but the procedures to put your record in order already exist, and one of them has a closing window.

If you live in Japan, you know the envelopes: National Pension (国民年金) slips, and National Health Insurance (国民健康保険, NHI) bills from your city. Some months got paid; some, during a job change or a lean season, did not.

The Japanese government has announced that this is changing. From June 2027, whether you have been paying pension and NHI premiums is expected to be taken into account when the Immigration Services Agency examines applications to renew or change a status of residence. The direction of the policy has been announced, but the final detailed rules — exactly how unpaid periods will be weighed — have not been fully published yet. No honest guide can predict your individual outcome.

What you can do, starting today, is move your record from “unpaid and untouched” to “officially in process.” That means an exemption application filed, an installment plan agreed, a consultation on record, and receipts kept — steps that cost nothing to start.

The short answer

  1. Get your real numbers first. Ask the pension office or check Nenkin Net for your pension record, and ask your city hall NHI section for your NHI balance. Do not guess from the pile of envelopes.
  2. For pension gaps, file for exemption or deferral now. Applications can cover periods up to 2 years and 1 month back — and that window closes month by month.
  3. For NHI arrears, consult your city hall. Reductions and installment payments (分納) are decided by your municipality, so only your own city counter can tell you what is available.
  4. Keep every document. Approval notices, installment agreements, payment slips and receipts are the proof you can actually show later.
  5. Start months before your renewal, not weeks. Applications take time to be processed and recorded.

Step 1: Find out exactly what you owe

For your pension, visit your local pension office (年金事務所) or the National Pension desk at city hall and ask for your payment record. You can also check online through Nenkin Net, the record service of the Japan Pension Service. For NHI, visit the NHI section (国民健康保険の窓口) at your city or ward office and ask for a statement of unpaid premiums. Bring your residence card and My Number card to both counters.

Ask the same question in both places: “From which month to which month am I unpaid?” In Japanese: Nan-nen nan-gatsu kara, nan-nen nan-gatsu made ga minō desu ka? (何年何月から何年何月までが未納ですか？). Write the answer down: this month-by-month map is what every later step works from.

Step 2: Pension — file the exemption before the window closes

If paying was difficult because of low income, job loss, or being a student, the National Pension has official routes: full or partial exemption (全額免除・一部免除), payment deferral (納付猶予), and the special student payment system (学生納付特例). The key fact, confirmed by the Japan Pension Service, is the retroactive window: you can apply for periods going back to 2 years and 1 month before the month your application is accepted. Every month you wait, the oldest month falls out of reach permanently — which is why this step is the urgent one.

Apply at the National Pension desk of your city hall or at a pension office; mail and Myna Portal applications are also available. One form covers one pension “year” (July to June), so two past years usually means two forms. If part of your gap is older than the window, ask the counter what options remain for those months. Approved exemption periods also count toward the qualifying period for the basic pension. To start, say: Nenkin hokenryō no menjo o shinsei shitai desu — “I’d like to apply for a pension premium exemption.”

Step 3: NHI — consult at city hall about reductions and installments

NHI is run by your municipality, so there is no single national arrears form and no national answer. Go to the NHI counter at your city or ward office and say you want to consult about payment: Hokenryō no shiharai ni tsuite sōdan shitai desu (保険料の支払いについて相談したいです). Depending on your situation and your city, the possibilities can include premium reductions for low income (軽減), relief measures in cases such as job loss or disaster (減免), and installment payments (分納) that split the balance into monthly amounts you can actually sustain.

What is available is decided by your municipality — which is why the consultation itself matters: it creates an official record that you engaged with the debt instead of ignoring it. A friend’s arrangement in another city tells you nothing about yours. If a plan is agreed, get it in writing and pay on the agreed dates. Ask: Bunkatsu de shiharau koto wa dekimasu ka? — “Can I pay in installments?”

Step 4: Build your proof file, and work backwards from renewal

Keep one folder — paper or scanned — with exemption approval notices, your installment agreement, every payment slip stub and receipt (領収書), and letters from the pension office or city hall. If you later need to demonstrate your payment situation, this file is the difference between explaining and proving.

Work backwards from your period of stay expiry. This month: get both balances and file the pension exemption, because its window moves against you daily. Next: hold the NHI consultation and start any plan. None of this guarantees a result at renewal. It replaces the weakest record — unpaid, unconsulted, undocumented — with the strongest available: applied, agreed, paying, and documented. For your specific case, consult the pension office, city hall, the Immigration Services Agency, or a certified immigration specialist (行政書士).

FAQ

Will unpaid premiums definitely cause a refusal from June 2027?

No one can say that yet. Payment status is expected to be taken into account from June 2027, but how unpaid periods will be weighed has not been fully published. Treat it as a reason to put your record in order, not a known outcome.

How far back can a pension exemption application go?

Up to 2 years and 1 month before the month your application is accepted, according to the Japan Pension Service. The window rolls forward every month, so filing sooner preserves more months.

Can I arrange NHI installments online or nationally?

NHI is administered by each municipality, so reductions and installment arrangements are handled at your own city or ward office counter. Terms differ by city; a consultation there is the only way to know yours.

What paperwork should I keep?

Approval notices, any written installment agreement, payment stubs and receipts, and official letters. Scan them the day you receive them.
