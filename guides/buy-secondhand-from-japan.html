Buying Second-Hand From Japan: Mercari Condition Grades, Proxy Fees and Fake Checks

The sold-out figure is on Mercari at half your local price — behind three Japanese phrases that machine translation flattens into the same cheerful English. One means “light wear, works fine.” Another means “untested, sold as scrap.” Here is the decoder.

You found it: the sold-out figure, the out-of-print game, the plush that never left Japan — on Mercari or in a BookOff listing at half the price sellers charge in your country. Then you open the description and hit a wall of Japanese: やや傷や汚れあり. 動作未確認. ノークレーム・ノーリターンでお願いします. One phrase means “light wear, works fine.” Another means “untested — buy it as scrap.” Machine translation flattens them into similar-sounding English, and the difference only shows up when the box lands on your doorstep, with no practical way to send it back.

This guide is the decoder: the condition language sellers actually use, why shop letter ranks cannot be compared between shops, the full proxy fee stack, and the checks that catch a fake before your money leaves.

The short answer

  1. Read the grade, then read the words and the photos. On Mercari, sellers pick one of six fixed condition grades — but the grade is the seller’s own judgement, not an inspection. The description and close-up photos are where the truth lives.
  2. Letter ranks (S / A / B / C) have no national standard. Each shop defines its own scale; an “A” from one store can match a “B” from another.
  3. Budget for four layers of cost, and assume no returns. Through a proxy you pay item price + domestic shipping + proxy service fee + international shipping — and possibly import tax or duty on top. Most Japanese second-hand sales are final.

Step 1: Decode the six Mercari condition grades

Mercari Japan — the marketplace most overseas collectors buy from — uses these six grades. The jump from the third to the fourth is where “looks clean” becomes “visibly used.”

未使用に近いLike new / almost unusedUsed a few times at most, little or no sign of use.

Step 2: Read the words sellers add on top of the grade

The grade is only the headline. Free-text words change what you receive. 美品 or 極美品 (“beautiful” / near-mint) is the seller’s praise — opinion, not a grade, to be trusted only with matching photos. 使用感あり warns that the item shows signs of use, whatever the grade says. 箱なし means no box and 箱傷み means the box is damaged — for figures and games, the box can be half the resale value. 欠品あり means something is missing. The phrase you want is 付属品完備 — all accessories included.

For anything that plugs in, two phrases decide the purchase. 動作確認済み means tested and working — the strongest wording for electronics. 動作未確認 means not tested: price it as if it does not work. And ジャンク means junk — sold as faulty, untested, or for parts; some junk items do work, but that is luck, not a description. Finally, 送料込み means shipping inside Japan is included, while 着払い means it is paid on delivery, which changes your proxy total. In physical shops such as BookOff and Hard Off, the same words appear on price tags.

Step 3: Translate letter ranks using that shop’s own scale

Many shops skip the six grades and stamp items N, S, A, B, C or D. A typical scale runs: N or S for new; A for very good; B for normal used, with visible light wear but fully usable; C for heavy wear; and D for damaged or junk. Some stores add SA, AB or BC steps. Treat that pattern as a pattern only, never a promise. Before you buy, open the shop’s “about condition ranks” page (状態ランクについて) and read its definitions. A rank with no published scale is decoration — judge by photos alone.

Step 4: Add up the real price — the proxy fee stack

Most Mercari sellers ship only within Japan, so overseas buyers usually order through a proxy (buying) service: the proxy buys the item, receives it at its warehouse, then forwards it to you. Your total is a stack: item price + domestic shipping + proxy service fee + international shipping — plus any import tax or duty your country charges on arrival.

Three practical points change the total. Service fees are charged per item or per order depending on the service, so five small listings can cost more in fees than one expensive one. Consolidating several items into one parcel usually cuts international shipping, but warehouse storage is free only for a limited window. And international shipping is priced on weight and box size, so a large, mostly empty figure box costs real money.

Step 5: Run the fake and failure checks before you pay

Counterfeits concentrate in figures, plush, trading cards and branded fashion. No single check proves authenticity, but fakes usually fail several of these. Price sanity: far below every other listing for the same item is itself a warning. Copyright and maker marks: licensed figures normally carry the copyright holder’s mark and a maker logo on the box; blurry printing or a missing mark are red flags. Photos of the actual item: stock photos only, or photos that avoid the box back, the base and the face, hide what you need to see. Seller history: a brand-new account selling only high-demand collectibles, with no ratings, deserves more caution than an established seller. For electronics, prefer 動作確認済み and treat every ジャンク item as non-working.

Finally, the returns reality. Private-seller marketplaces in Japan run on ノークレーム・ノーリターン — “no claims, no returns” — and a proxy between you and the seller makes disputes slower still. Buy as if the sale is final, because it usually is. Before you pay, you can ask: 状態を詳しく教えてください (Jōtai o kuwashiku oshiete kudasai — “Please tell me more about the condition”).

FAQ

Is an “A rank” item from one shop the same as an “A” from another?

No. There is no shared S/A/B/C standard in Japan; a rank means only what that one shop says it means.

A listing says ジャンク but the photos look fine. Is it a bargain?

Treat it as non-working. “Junk” means faulty, untested or for parts. Some junk items do work — but that is luck, not a description.

How much extra will a proxy add to the price?

Expect four layers: the item, domestic shipping, the proxy’s service fee (per item or per order), and international shipping by weight and box size — plus possible import tax or duty on arrival.

Can I return an item that arrives worse than described?

Usually not. Between private sellers, sales run on “no claims, no returns,” and a proxy narrows disputes further. Resolve every doubt before payment.
